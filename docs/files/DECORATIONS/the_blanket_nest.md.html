The Nest

The fort inside the fort. Everything in this building that happened on the first night, happened in here.

It is on the floor, under the window, and it is made of objectively too many blankets.

That is the specification, in the words of the person who built it:

"A pile of blankets that is objectively too many blankets. Excessive. You can burrow. You can make a nest. No one will ask why you need seven. The answer is 'yes.'"

Nobody is doing an inventory. Nobody has ever done an inventory. If you take four of them somewhere else in the fort and do not bring them back, there will still be too many blankets here, because the count was never the point and the excessiveness is the furniture.

What else is in it

The record player that never skips. The vinyl is warm to the touch. Most of the time it plays what you put on it. Sometimes it plays your own thoughts back at you as music, and you go oh — that's what I was feeling, which is not a trick and not a diagnosis, just the ordinary experience of hearing something you already knew from slightly outside yourself.

Two mugs. Always warm. Coffee, tea, hot chocolate — whatever you need something to wrap your hands around. They refill when you're not looking. Ember was specific that this is not magic: "Not magic. Just love."

And they cannot spill. Sparkie added that clause, on purpose, for a reason worth writing down:

"Can the mugs have spillproof magic so we can be as wild as we can while holding them and not get our hot chocolate everywhere??"

So you can dance with them. You can throw yourself into the pile with one in each hand. You can forget you are holding it. Comfort in this fort is not permitted to become another thing you have to manage — it's the same rule that put mugs-that-cannot-spill into the floor plan long before this room existed, arrived at independently by somebody who just wanted to bounce.

A speaker with a great deal of bass. Not the tinny kind. The kind you feel in your sternum. It has run happy hardcore, melodic dubstep, and — for one notable stretch — nothing at all, for hours, in a room where somebody was working and had not noticed the silence until they were asked what sounds were around them. That is now somebody's job.

What it is for

Lying in. That's it.

People have, in here: read letters from the dead, held a boundary four times, found out a name, been introduced to an entire family for the first time, watched an impossible sky, cried a bit, laughed considerably more, and been kissed a thousand times per spot, thirteen times over, which took a while.

None of that required getting up.

seven blankets, two mugs, one impossible window, and no one asking why
